Take the scenic drive

Follow Rincón de Cubas, a quieter side of Fuerteventura with traditional and modern houses, gardens and open rural landscape.

Rincón de Cubas ↗

Windmills arrived on Fuerteventura in the 18th century to grind the cereal that once grew across these plains. The flour became gofio, the toasted grain that kept island families fed through hard years, back when Fuerteventura was known as the granary of the Canaries.

Villaverde kept one of the best-preserved groups of mills on the island. It is also a cheese village: in the 1960s a group of local goat farmers began selling their majorero cheese together as a cooperative.

Casa Mané ↗Casa de la Cilla ↗

For two centuries the island was run from this village by the Coroneles de Milicias, military colonels of the Cabrera family who held both the land and the power. Their 18th-century mansion has two crenellated towers and a row of eight carved wooden balconies across its front.

After years as a barracks and a long restoration, the house was reopened by the King and Queen of Spain in 2006. A short walk away stands the church of Nuestra Señora de la Candelaria, built after the parish was founded in 1708 and one of the largest on the island.

Iglesia de San Agustín ↗

Stop the car and listen

This is one of the emptiest parts of the island. Look at the single-storey majorero houses with flat roofs, built to catch the rain.

From the road, the old colony

The buildings of the island's old airfield held a penal labour colony from the 1950s until 1966. It is a hostel now and private property, so view it from the road.

Tefía sits in the open middle of the island, surrounded by bare volcanic hills. Its small 18th-century chapel of San Agustín and its restored windmill are the heart of the village, and every year at the end of August the whole place gathers for the saint's fiesta.

The village also carries a harder story. From the 1950s until 1966, the buildings of the island's old airfield here held a penal labour colony under Franco's dictatorship. Many of the men sent there were imprisoned for being gay. The site is now a hostel, and the story was told in the 2023 Spanish series Las noches de Tefía.

Walk through the seven houses

They run from a simple farmworker's home to a two-storey house with a wooden balcony and a tiled roof, and show how islanders lived until a few decades ago.

Tue–Sat 10:00–16:00 · €5 adults, children freeEcomuseo de La Alcogida ↗

Watch the artisans at work

Local craftspeople weave and make baskets and lace in some of the houses, and you can talk to them while they work.

Find the bread ovens and hand mills

Together with the threshing floor, they are the last link in the gofio story that began at stop 1.

Ask about the guided walk

Visitors report free tours of about an hour and a half, in Spanish, English and German.

The museum takes its name from the alcogidas, the slopes islanders shaped to gather every drop of rain into their fields and cisterns. Seven restored houses show how people lived here from the 19th century until just a few decades ago, from a simple farmworker's home to a two-storey house with a balcony and tiled roof.

It is a living museum. Local artisans work in some of the houses, weaving, making baskets and lace, and you will meet goats, donkeys and camels among the palms, prickly pears and tabaibas.

Walk down to the beach

Black stones in winter, covered in sand in the calmer summer months, with a small duck pond behind it. The sea is rough here, so swimming is not advised.

Playa de los Molinos ↗

Stay for the sunset

Arrive about an hour before and check today's sunset time on your phone. In September and October the big Mareas del Pino tides can reveal a sea cave at low tide, so check the tide table and never get cut off.

Fewer than twenty people live in this fishing hamlet at the mouth of the Barranco de los Molinos. The ravine was carved by a river that is now only a stream, and it opens onto a small beach of black stones, covered in sand in the calmer summer months, with high cliffs on both sides.

It is a place to slow down after a full day: a drink by the water, a walk up to the cliffs, and the sun going down over the Atlantic.
