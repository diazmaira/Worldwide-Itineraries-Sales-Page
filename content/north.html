1 Corralejo · Stop 1 of 5

Sand Dunes

Barefoot in a sea of pale sand.

~30 min on footEasy
Navigate here

What to do here 4 things · tap a number

Park along the FV-1 and walk straight in

Pull into one of the free lay-bys along the coast road. There are no gates and no ticket office: the dunes begin where the tarmac ends.

Your photo here
Corralejo Natural Park ↗

Take your shoes off

The sand is fine and clean, and walking barefoot is half the pleasure. By mid-afternoon it gets hot, so test it first.

Your photo here

Climb to a ridge for the view

Pick one of the higher crests. From the top you get the whole dune field on one side, and the Atlantic with Isla de Lobos and Lanzarote beyond on the other.

Your photo here
~15 min up and back

Have fun with the camera

Footprints running off into the sand, a jump shot against the sky, someone small against a big empty ridge. The light is best early or late.

Your photo here

These dunes are not desert sand blown over from Africa. Most of the grains are ground-up shells and other marine remains, washed ashore and then pushed inland by the trade winds over thousands of years. The field is a protected natural park, and it is still moving.

What does come from Africa is the calima: a hot easterly wind that carries Saharan dust across the islands. It turns the sky milky, hides the horizon and can last several days. Harmless for a short walk, but the views disappear.

Good to know

Stay off the fragile plants that hold the sand together, take your rubbish with you, and leave the dunes exactly as you found them.

Corralejo Old Town

Popcorn, a small harbour and a marina.

~60 min returnEasy
Navigate here

What to do here 5 things · tap a number

Start at Popcorn Beach

The white nuggets that look like popcorn are rhodoliths: fossilised coralline algae built up over thousands of years. They are protected, so look, photograph and leave every piece where it is.

Corralejo began as a handful of fishermen's houses facing a sheltered bay, and that old core is still there behind the newer seafront. Walking it takes under an hour and gives you the town in the right order: the strange beach first, then the working harbour, then the marina.

Good to know

Popcorn Beach has no shade and no facilities. Take water, and keep vehicles off the stones.

3 Volcano · Stop 3 of 5

Calderón Hondo

~60 min returnEasy–moderate
Navigate here

What to do here 4 things · tap a number

Start from the marked trail

The path leaves from the edge of Lajares and is signposted. It climbs steadily on a wide track, with no scrambling.

Your photo here
Free · ~30 min upCalderón Hondo trail ↗

Walk the rim to the viewpoint

A railed viewpoint looks straight down into the crater. From there you can see the chain of cones running north, the dunes, Lobos and, on a clear day, Lanzarote.

Your photo here

Look out for the ground squirrels

Barbary ground squirrels are common along the path and come close. Please do not feed them: they are an introduced species and human food harms them.

Your photo here

Go early or late

There is no shade anywhere on the trail. Early morning and the last hours of light are cooler and far better for photographs.

Your photo here

Calderón Hondo is one of the youngest volcanoes on Fuerteventura, part of a short chain of cones at the northern tip of the island. Its crater is still clean-edged, which is why it reads so clearly from the rim: most of the island's older volcanoes have been worn flat.

From the top the whole north opens up at once, which is why this stop sits in the middle of the day rather than at the end.

Good to know

The ground is loose volcanic gravel, so wear proper shoes and take short steps coming down. The wind at the rim can be strong: stay behind the railing and away from the crater edge.

4 El Cotillo · Stop 4 of 5

El Cotillo Lagoons & Lighthouse

Turquoise pools and a lighthouse at the point.

~60 minEasy
Navigate here

What to do here 4 things · tap a number

Walk down to the lagoons

A line of shallow pools sheltered by volcanic rock, just north of El Cotillo. The water is calm and clear, and the colour is the reason people come.

Your photo here
Free parkingEl Cotillo lagoons ↗

Swim or snorkel if the sea is calm

On a quiet day the pools are warm and shallow enough for children, with small fish among the rocks. Look at the water before you go in.

Your photo here

Find a corralito

The low circular stone walls along the shore are hand-built wind shelters. Sit inside one and the trade wind passes over your head.

Your photo here

Walk north to El Tostón lighthouse

Three towers of different ages stand together at the point, with a traditional fishing museum inside. Check the current opening hours before you go.

Your photo here
~20 min walk from the lagoonsEl Tostón lighthouse ↗

The lagoons sit behind a broken rim of volcanic rock that takes the force of the Atlantic and leaves the water behind it almost still. It is one of the few places on this coast where you can swim comfortably.

Conditions change with the season. In winter the swell is bigger and the wind stronger, and the pools empty and fill quickly with the tide.

Good to know

There are no lifeguards. If the swell is up, enjoy the colour from the rocks instead of swimming, and never turn your back on the ocean.

El Cotillo Old Town

Canarian tapas on the harbour wall while the sun slips into the Atlantic.

~1.5 hoursEasy

Park at the Castillo de El Tostón

The round watchtower above the old harbour has free parking beside it, and is the natural start of the walk.

Walk through the old town

Down past the old harbour and back up through the narrow streets. It is small, and an unhurried loop takes about half an hour.

Sit down for tapas

Three places the guide recommends: La Vaca Azul by the old harbour, Azul y Sal and Cafetería Aguayre.

La Vaca Azul ↗Azul y Sal ↗Cafetería Aguayre ↗

Order like a local

Pimientos de Padrón, papas arrugadas with mojo, majorero cheese, croquettes, fresh fish, and paella if you are a group.

Your photo here
~€25 per person for three tapas and a drink

Stay for the sunset

El Cotillo faces west, so the day ends here. Arrive about an hour before and take a table, or a rock, with a clear view of the water.

Your photo here

El Cotillo keeps the shape of a fishing village: a small harbour, a tower built to watch for raiders, and a handful of streets behind it. It is the right place to finish, because everything you have seen today faces this coast.

Eat early by Spanish standards if you want a table at sunset, or eat late and watch the light go first.

Good to know

Book ahead at weekends and in high season. Opening days vary out of season, so check before you drive.
